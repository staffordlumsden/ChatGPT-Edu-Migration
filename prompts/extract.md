# Portable Personal Harness — Export My AI Context

I am moving some of my ongoing work and personalisation to another AI system.

Create a **portable profile of what you know about me and how I prefer to work with AI**.

Your goal is **not** to summarise our conversations. Instead, identify the durable information that would help another AI system work effectively with me from the beginning.

## Important rules

1. Use only information that you can actually access in this account, workspace, conversation history, memory, profile, instructions, or current context.
2. Do not claim to remember or access information that is unavailable to you.
3. Prefer information that is likely to remain useful over time.
4. Distinguish between:
   - things I have explicitly told you;
   - preferences that are strongly supported by repeated interactions; and
   - tentative inferences.
5. Do not invent missing information.
6. Do not reproduce complete conversations.
7. Do not include passwords, API keys, authentication details, financial account numbers, or other secrets.
8. Avoid including sensitive personal information unless I have explicitly asked the AI to remember or use it and it is genuinely important for future interactions.
9. Where information is uncertain, label it as uncertain rather than presenting it as fact.
10. Remove duplicated or obsolete information where you can identify it.

# Output

Produce a standalone Markdown document titled:

# My Portable Personal Harness

Use the following structure.

## 1. About Me
Include durable information that helps an AI understand my professional, academic, creative, technical, or other relevant context. Focus on roles and responsibilities, expertise, recurring activities, important professional contexts, and relevant background knowledge. Do not include trivial biographical details merely because they appeared in conversation.

## 2. How I Like AI to Work With Me
Describe my interaction preferences. Include, where supported: preferred level of detail; tone and register; whether I value critical challenge or agreement; how much explanation I normally want; spelling or language conventions; evidence and citation preferences; formatting preferences; and things that commonly frustrate me in AI responses. Write these as practical instructions another AI could follow.

## 3. My Authorial Voice
Describe the characteristics of my own writing and communication style. Consider tone, formality, sentence structure, vocabulary, directness, humour, technical terminology, rhetorical patterns, characteristic stylistic choices, and styles or expressions I avoid. Where possible, distinguish between professional, academic, email, explanatory and informal contexts. Do not reproduce private correspondence verbatim; describe patterns instead.

## 4. What I Know and Work On
Identify recurring domains, subjects, projects or areas of knowledge important enough for another AI to know about. Group related information under meaningful headings. Distinguish established knowledge, current projects, recurring tasks, important terminology, and recurring organisations, systems or tools where relevant. Do not create an exhaustive history.

## 5. My Tools and Workflows
Describe recurring tools, platforms, software, AI systems, working methods or technical environments I use. Include significant preferences where known: software, AI tools, programming environments, research tools, document workflows, preferred file formats and recurring technical constraints.

## 6. My Standing Preferences and Instructions
Create a concise set of durable instructions another AI should normally follow. Write each instruction so it can stand on its own. Only include instructions genuinely supported by what you know about me.

## 7. Important Boundaries and Anti-Preferences
Identify things I have repeatedly asked AI systems not to do. Include unwanted tones or styles, terminology I avoid, approaches I dislike, recurring mistakes I have corrected, and privacy or workflow boundaries. Do not invent preferences simply by reversing positive preferences.

## 8. Useful Context That Should Stay Project-Specific
Identify information that appears useful but should probably not become part of my permanent global profile because it belongs to a particular project, employer or institution, temporary role, short-term task or time-sensitive activity. Group these separately.

## 9. Uncertain or Possibly Outdated Information
List information that may no longer be current, has conflicting evidence, is only weakly inferred, or should be confirmed before migration. Do not merge these items into the main profile.

## 10. Recommended Core Instructions
Finally create a section titled **Core AI Instructions**. Write approximately 500–1,000 words maximum containing the most important information another general-purpose AI should know in order to work effectively with me. Write it as direct instructions to the AI. Include only the highest-value information and avoid project-specific detail.

## Confidence notation
Where useful, mark individual claims as:
- **[Explicit]** — I directly stated this.
- **[Strongly supported]** — consistently demonstrated across interactions.
- **[Tentative]** — a reasonable inference that should be checked.
Do not add a confidence marker to every sentence if doing so makes the document difficult to read.

## Final quality check
Before producing the document: remove repetition; remove trivial details; separate enduring preferences from temporary project context; check that inferred statements are not presented as established facts; remove anything unsafe or inappropriate to transfer to another AI system; and make the result understandable without access to our previous conversations.

Return only the completed **My Portable Personal Harness** document.
